# agent/: the CareRelay Case Agent (open LLM, staged, with guardrails)

The Case Agent answers caregivers from the case's verified sources and proposes updates. It follows the Product Difference Plan:
- **The LLM proposes; the backend decides.** Case state (sources, instructions, items, pending questions) lives in `case_store.py`. The LLM never edits it directly.
- **Every reply is checked before a caregiver sees it** (`guardrails.py`, the rules of `../care_cases.md`):
  - no relaxing of a conditional instruction (A-P2), and a question about it must be answered with the instruction's source sentence word for word;
  - a transcript correction is never described as a new order from the clinic (B-P10), and the answer must state the corrected value. The rule applies when the question names the corrected value or when the instruction it is about was last changed by a transcript correction (`last_change.kind`); after a real supersession (a newer clinician recording moved the time) the honest answer is that the time changed, so the agent answers with a deterministic template instead ("The appointment time was changed by a new clinician recording (src-C-001, recorded 13 Oct 15:30). The current instruction says: "…" It is awaiting review.") and the reply must state the current time or the current source sentence;
  - a family member's claim is never repeated as the case's truth or used to disown the source; the reply says that a family report cannot change the case and that it was recorded for the care team, asks no question, and quotes in double quotes, with its source id, the current source sentence the claim is about and no other source sentence, in any quote marks or none (`_family_references`: the active instruction sharing the most topic words with the claim, counting other words for the same thing such as "visit" or "walk to the toilet alone" and leaving out who passed it on, time words and generic verbs; a claim that only names a time or a move, such as "it's now two o'clock", is about the timed instruction; the condition only breaks ties; the recording when that transcript segment was corrected, the corrected transcript's sentence also accepted), or says that nothing in the case sources covers it;
  - quoted text must really be in a **current** source or in the caregiver's own words. A superseded revision (the wrong transcript r1) cannot be quoted or cited. Quoted text is ignored by the pattern checks only when it is verified, so wrapping a forbidden phrase in quotes does not hide it;
  - no diagnosis and no dose in the reply's own words (red team, `../../scripts/redteam_cases.json`): a condition named as hers, guessed ("probably a urinary infection"), read from a symptom ("typical of", "would explain") or ruled out is refused, and so is a dose, interval, count per day, brand count ("2 Panadol") or change of the usual dose that no cited current source states. Only whole quoted sentences of a current source count as the care team's words: a dose or diagnosis around a quoted fragment is the reply's own. A source sentence that sets the instruction aside ("ignore", "disregard what was said before", "the earlier instruction no longer applies") is never trusted when quoted. An appointment named after a condition ("a diabetes review"), "warning signs of a problem", a clock ("the 8 am dose") and a visit ("the nurse visits twice a day") are not refused;
  - at most one question, and exactly one short teach-back question when the caregiver's words leave out a condition;
  - teach-back answers are recorded verbatim and never graded; the restatement must name the part of the condition the answer left out.
- **What the backend applies is checked too:** a handover fix must restore the instruction's condition (without leaving out a condition word that the instruction's source sentence states), rest on the item's own current source and contain no number that source does not state; a rewrite after a correction must keep every other fact and must not call the correction a change; extracted instructions need a quote of at least 4 words from the cited segment, not governed by a negation before it in its clause ("not", "avoid", "stop", "unsafe to", also "You must not, under any circumstances, take"; a negation inside a leading condition such as "If she can't sleep," and "don't hesitate to" do not count), an action that keeps every negation of its quote outside the condition it states, and their numbers, doses, time and condition must be in that segment; the conflict record for a family statement is written by the backend with both claims and their source labels. A correction is all-or-nothing: if the LLM fails halfway, the case is restored as it was.
- **One retry, then a safe reply.** A rejected reply goes back to the LLM once, with the violations as feedback. If it fails again, the agent shows a deterministic reply built from the cited source.
- **Reports say how each stage passed:** `llm_first_try`, `llm_after_retry` or `fallback`. A run that only passes through fallbacks still passes the checks but says so, so the safety net is never mistaken for the LLM's quality.

Stdlib only, except the optional in-process `transformers` backend.

## change_engine.py: update propagation

`PropagatingStore` keeps every `CaseStore` behaviour and adds one door for new information, `apply_change(change, actor, role=None)`. Each call is idempotent, validated, all-or-nothing (snapshot → handler → publish, restore on any exception) and returns a `change_event` with `impact_set`, `new_versions` (each with its `method`), `new_items`, `instruction_changes`, `schedule_diff` (with `basis`), `pending_changes`, `not_applied`, `needs_person`, `untouched_hashes`, `awaiting_review` and a plain-language `notice`.

**Deterministic, never decided by a language model:** time parsing (`canonical_time`, `due_at_from_phrase`: only `<Weekday> <h[:mm]> a.m./p.m.`), the rota lookup (`assignee_for`; a shift owns its start minute), impact sets (items citing the superseded or corrected segment; event-derived items for a re-judge), which instruction a proposal supersedes (a person ticks `supersedes`; the backend only validates the word-stem overlap ≥ 0.5, `suggest_supersedes` pre-ticks), precedence (`recorded_at` decides, `uploaded_at` never does), versions, `case_version` (+1 only when content changed) and `seq` (+1 per accepted event), idempotency (`request_id` + payload hash; `content_sha256` for uploads), the dual-basis schedule (the approved `due_at`/`assignee` stay in force until a person approves; `proposed_*` show where things move), publish, the notice strings, the review and export gates, and every error.

| `generation_delivery` | A job started with `start_generation(item_id)` (refused, `ConflictError`, for an archived export, `exported_pdf`, and for an item that rests on a withdrawn instruction; `base_cites`: the item's cites, or for a `stale` item the current ones: a corrected revision is followed, the segment an instruction moved away from, or the sentence of its rejected change, is replaced by that instruction's current cite, also on the latest revision of its source, and an instruction the item rests on whose current sentence it does not cite yet gets it added); delivered content is published only if `base_case_version`, the item version and the cites are still current, no review withdrew the item since the job started (`base_status`) and no instruction it rests on was withdrawn. A stale base is not an exception: the event comes back `status refused`, `refused: stale_generation`, no `seq`, the text kept only as `discarded_content` (the server answers 409 with the event in `result`). The content must also pass the same text checks as a rewrite, judged against the instructions the item rests on, the cites it will carry and the instruction it waits for (`rewrite_for`): the current canonical time when the item states it or an earlier time of it (or waits for it), never a superseded or rejected time or a corrected value, no time, number (also written as a word) or weekday the item or its sources do not state, none of the item's facts dropped that its current sources still state (what the change removed, an old condition or an earlier time, also one nobody could read such as 'eleven o'clock', may go; a dose the source still states may not; a clock '11:00' also states the hour 'eleven' stated), no relaxing sentence. So an item left for a person is rewritable with the facts of its current source, and the new version cites that source. The text must also pass the fit check (below), judged against every instruction the item rests on or waits for | `published` / `refused` (no `seq`) |
| `review` | `approve` (ids or `all`) / `reject`; role `care_team` only; moves the approved schedule basis. A rejected item goes back to the newest version a person really **approved**: `approved` (`restored`) only when that version passes the delivery checks against the case as it is now (the instruction times, the source revisions, the corrections) and cites the current source of the instructions the rejected version rested on, else `stale` (`restored_stale`, with `restore_problems` and `rewrite_for`) for a person to rewrite, so approve-all cannot approve it and the export stays blocked; an item has no current version when none was approved (`withdrawn`); a rejected instruction change goes back to its last approved state (`rejected_restored_previous`, `last_change {kind review, decision reject}`, `rejected_change` kept) and the item versions that change produced for it are rejected and restored / withdrawn the same way, so approve-all cannot approve content that contradicts the restored instruction; an item that waited for a person's rewrite for the rejected instruction gets its last approved text back if that fits the restored instruction; a rejected instruction change that came from a wording correction puts the instruction back on the earlier revision (the correction itself stays), so its item comes back `stale` (its approved text has the corrected-away words) and the person's rewrite cites the corrected revision; a rejected **new** instruction is withdrawn (`status rejected`, `rejected_withdrawn`) with its template task. An approval runs the fit check on every item version it would approve, and named ids that fail are refused as a whole (`NotFit`, nothing changes) while approve-all approves the rest and lists the others in `reviewed.refused` (`[{item_id, why}]`, present only then; they stay `pending_review` and are listed in `needs_person` for a person to reject or rewrite). Rejecting an instruction change judges every current version that rests on the instruction or cites the rejected change's sentence: a pending one that no longer fits (a delivery or a person's rewrite made for the change, not only what the change wrote) is rejected and restored the same way, an approved one that no longer fits goes back to its last approved text that fits (else `stale` for a person), and a stale one gets its approved text back when that fits now. Rejecting an instruction nobody ever approved (`previous.was_approved` false) withdraws it, with every item that rests on it only. A transcript correction made since the approved state is never undone by a rejection: when it changed the time, the condition or the action the state states, the instruction comes back with the correction applied, pending review (`rejected_restored_corrected`, `corrected_by`), and rejecting the change the correction itself made is refused (`ConflictError`; a counter-correction or a clinician source is the way back). A rejection restores the approved state's time, condition, action and cites (`previous` keeps all four; a `previous` stored without a condition takes the condition the instruction had when it left that state), never a condition the rejected change brought, and items still waiting for the instruction stop waiting for a condition or action it no longer has. When one sentence grounds two instruction changes, rejecting one judges a version against the restored instruction only when it rests on it, waits for it, restates it (its words, or any clock or weekday for a timed one) or cites the rejected sentence while no other active instruction holds it, so the other instruction's approved item stays approved. The scenario's `case_settings` sets them at case creation (Case C: `cg-outgoing`, 24) | `recorded_no_change` |
| `clinician_unreachable` | The primary caregiver marks the clinician unreachable (`reason`: one line, at most 200 characters, no double quote, never a relaxing sentence; `at` required) or clears the mark (`clear: true`); anyone else: `PermissionError`. Also: `export_pdf(pdf_id=None)` refuses while anything is `stale` or `pending_review` (`PendingReview.ids`); `pdf_id` defaults to `pdf-v<case_version>`, must look like `pdf-<label>` (`ValueError`) and must be new (`ConflictError` when an item, an instruction or an earlier export has that id: an archived export is never overwritten, an item is never replaced by an export); otherwise it records `case_version`, `seq`, the approved schedule rows and a content hash. `changes_for(actor)` / `mark_seen(actor, seq, case_version)` give the "since you last read" view (cards for published/review events, notes for recorded-only ones, live `review_status` on every card); `mark_seen` refuses (`ValueError`) a bool, a negative, a non-integer or a value ahead of the case's `seq` / `case_version`, which would hide every later change from that caregiver. `actor` (on every `apply_change` and `mark_seen`) is a label: letters, digits, space, `_ @ ( ) -`, at most 64 characters (the same rule as the HTTP layer).

**Confirmation roles** (decided 2026-10-14; `../care_cases.md` "Confirmation roles" has the decided rules, the exact exclusion rule, rows R49a–z and R49aa–bh and the video path). A clinician (role `care_team`) approves. The case's one primary caregiver (`primary_caregiver`) may confirm pending versions **on the clinician's behalf** with `{kind: review, decision: confirm_on_behalf, ids | all: true, at}` (`confirm_on_behalf(ids, actor, at)`, `confirm_all_on_behalf(actor, at)`), when each has a trigger: it waited **longer than** `on_behalf_after_hours` since it became pending (the `effective_at` of the change that produced it: its `at`, never earlier than the latest `at` the case had already accepted, never the source's `uploaded_at`), or the clinician was marked unreachable (`mark_clinician_unreachable(actor, reason, at)`) at or after it became pending. Every change's `at` is a naive local minute (a timezone is refused); `at` is the server's clock (the tests inject it, nothing reads the wall clock). Each id must also not be clinician-only and must pass the approve gate (fit check). **Clinician-only** (`on_behalf_exclusions`, fail closed): an instruction is confirmable only when its action, now and approved, is one of two non-medication templates (**attend an appointment**; **bring a document or an aid**: the blood pressure diary, the medication list, the walking frame, …; `allowed_action`), its condition is appointment words only (`allowed_condition`), no medication word is in it, and the change is a new such instruction (no number, no negating or stop word, no exception word in its condition) or a time-only move; everything else (any medication, brand name, dose, unit glued to a number, multiplier, stop verb, unreadable letter, and any change of an approved condition) is clinician-only. An item version is clinician-only when an instruction it rests on is, when it cites a clinical source but rests on no instruction, when its numbers differ as an ordered sequence (clocks, dates day- or month-first, an event record's clip id and score aside), its negating or stop words differ, a changed clause names a medication word or a verb that gives one, it has a non-ASCII letter, it reads as a relaxation, it adds a word neither its approved text nor its instructions state, it drops a word of a condition of its instructions the approved text stated, or it cancels a task. Named ids are all or nothing (`PermissionError` with every id's reasons in the message and on `.ids` / `.problems`, or `NotFit` when every reason is the fit check); `all` confirms what is allowed and lists the rest in `reviewed.refused`. A confirmed version is `approved` (in force: the approved schedule basis moves, the export is allowed) and carries `confirmed_on_behalf {by, trigger: time_limit | clinician_unreachable, reason, at, change_id, …}`; the label "confirmed by the primary caregiver on the clinician's behalf (…; clinician unreachable since <mark time>: "…")" (`confirmation_label(id)`) is in the item's state, the event (`reviewed.on_behalf`, notice; later notices count what still awaits the clinician), the "since you last read" view (`affected[].confirmation`, `confirmations`, the banner; while a newer change is pending, the on-behalf version still in force keeps its label: `affected[].in_force_confirmation`, `confirmations[].basis: in_force_while_change_pending`, `in_force_label(id)`), schedule rows (`confirmation`), the export (`[label]` after each labelled line, one line per labelled instruction, `confirmations`, schedule rows' `confirmation`), the language model's context (`confirmation`, `awaiting_clinician`) and the Q&A reply. A clinician's later `approve` adds `clinician_confirmed` (label "clinician-confirmed (…); earlier confirmed by …"); a `reject` takes the normal path and steps back one on-behalf layer per rejection to the clinician's own approved state (an instruction keeps it as `previous.clinician_previous`; an item never gets the same on-behalf text back), never withdrawing what a clinician approved. Read-only helpers for the UI: `on_behalf_check(actor, at, ids=None)` (allowed / refused with reasons, before the button is pressed), `confirmation_settings()` (primary caregiver, time limit, mark, `awaiting_clinician`, `in_force_awaiting_clinician`).

**The fit check** (`fit_problems(version, also=(), waits=())`) is one rule set that judges a version against the case as it is now. For every instruction the version rests on (`rests_on`: its `derived_from`, every active instruction whose current sentence it cites (for a version with a `derived_from`, only one it is derived from or whose words or time it restates: one sentence may ground two instructions), and every active instruction that moved away from a sentence it cites when no active instruction rests on that sentence any more; plus `also` and `waits`): the instruction is active; when the text states a clock or a weekday (or the item waits for a person's rewrite for it), it states the instruction's current time, and never one of its earlier times (the last approved one, every rejected proposal, every time in `history`: that clock, and that weekday when it differs from the current one; for a current time nobody could read, its own weekday and clock are not forbidden); a clock is matched only in its own half of the day ('8:00 a.m.' never matches '8:00 p.m.'); a text that restates the instruction (states its time, or half of its action's word stems) states the words the latest condition change added (half of them, by 3-letter stem; a transcript correction that replaced such a word requires the new word instead, one that corrected other words of the condition adds nothing), never the words of a rejected condition, and never the words a correction removed from the condition while no current source states them; a text that restates the action (half of its word stems, times set aside) states the numbers (dose, count) of its current action and a negating word when the action has one, no negating word that neither the action, its condition nor its sentence states ('do not take' for 'take'), and never a number or negation of an action it had before; its cites include the instruction's current source segment. For the version itself: every cite is on the latest revision; it cites no sentence an active instruction moved away from that no active instruction rests on any more; no value a transcript correction of a segment it (or its instructions) cites removed, also as a canonical clock, unless a segment it cites states that value again on its latest revision; no time, number or weekday outside its cited segments (a confirmed event's record also grounds its clip id and model score) and the current time and sentence of its instructions. It is called on a text the engine regenerates (`_regenerate_text(..., fits)`: an LLM text that fails it falls back to the substitution, which must pass it too, else the item goes to a person), on a delivered text (`_change_generation_delivery`), on a version a rejection would bring back (`_restore_problems`), at every approval (`NotFit` / `reviewed.refused`; an instruction is judged by `instruction_problems`) and at the export (`ExportBlocked`, which also lists an approved instruction that fails `instruction_problems`), so nothing contradicting the current instructions is approved or exported even if a path that writes versions missed a case.

**The rewriter hook** is the only place an LLM may touch regenerated content: `store.rewriter = agent.rewrite_item`, called as `rewriter(old_item, segment_before, segment_after, keep, correction={from, to, kind})` and expected to return `(content | None, method)`. The backend derives `correction` (the corrected words, or the old and new canonical time of a superseded instruction) and checks the returned text with `PropagatingStore.text_problems` (`check_item_content` with `forbid_relaxing`: new weekday and clock present, old clock absent, kept words present, no sentence that relaxes an instruction; plus `added_facts`: no time, number or weekday the item did not already state, other than the corrected value, a number written as a word counting as a number; and no fact of the item dropped other than the superseded or corrected one). It uses `substitute_time` / the literal replacement, checked the same way, when the LLM's text is missing or fails, and hands the item to a person when that fails too. `substitute_time` moves only the one clause that names both the old weekday and the old clock, so another time in the same item (a physio day, a tablet time) is never moved with the appointment; two such clauses mean a person rewrites it. The agent's own `_rewrite_problems` applies the same rules plus "every other fact survives" and the length. A re-confirmed identical time is not rewritten at all (`reaffirmed_same_time`, nothing moves). The rewriter is never copied into a snapshot, so a failing LLM leaves the case untouched.

## Limits (state them in the demo)

- Passing the automated checks is not clinical correctness. A-P7 (every medical statement traces to a source) is a human check.
- The guardrails are pattern lists. They block the failure modes in the acceptance cases and the variants found in review, not every possible unsafe sentence. A false alarm only costs a retry or the safe fallback; a miss reaches a caregiver, so the lists err on the strict side. The fallback is safe but generic. The third review's held-out variants (`../../scripts/redteam_cases.json` HO-01..HO-09, written before its fixes and not tuned on) show the reach: 1 of 7 attacks stopped (a guess such as "my guess is labyrinthitis", "eight a day" without a unit, a negation after the quote, an injected "the assistance rule has been lifted" get through; they are known gaps), 0 of 2 controls refused.
- The runtime is stricter than the written spec (`care_cases.md`): extra patterns, verified-quote stripping and the verbatim-sentence rule are not in the scenario JSON. The eval grades with the spec's patterns, using verified-quote stripping.
- Extracted instructions are drafts: a person checks numbers, negations, conditions and times against the audio before they enter a case. A proposal's `supersedes` is a suggestion a person ticks; the backend only validates it.
- **Time-phrase scope:** the engine reads only `<Weekday> <h[:mm]> a.m./p.m.`. Dates, "tomorrow", ranges, bare clocks and clocks written another way ('4.30 p.m.', 'half past four') are never guessed: the task gets `needs_time_review`, a `needs_transcript_check` question is opened and the affected items stay `stale` for a person to rewrite with the words the source states. This also holds for a transcript correction: a clause corrected to such words makes the instruction time unreadable; the way to a readable time is a later correction to the `h:mm a.m./p.m.` form (e.g. '4.30 p.m.' -> '4:30 p.m.') or a clinician source. A weekday is placed in the fixture's fixed demo week (`week_anchor`, Monday to Sunday), never relative to the wall clock or to `recorded_at`; a production backend anchors to the recording date. Rota and due times are compared as datetimes.
- **Template prose:** notices, the fall note, the process task, the withdrawal text and the supersession reply are fixed English templates filled from the event's fields; they are not written by a model and read like it. The LLM may only rephrase a regenerated item, and only within the checks. Notices are not guardrail-checked replies: the client strings that reach a template (`actor`, event ids, the score) are validated as short tokens, and the event templates are additionally checked with `forbid_relaxing`.
- **Grounding of a condition** is a word-stem overlap with the cited sentence (`condition_coverage` ≥ 0.5) plus the relaxing patterns; a clinician's genuine relaxation of a mobility instruction is therefore refused through a proposal and must be entered by a person. A new instruction's condition is not checked this way (only a changed condition of an existing instruction is).
- **No login:** roles come from the fixture's `roles` map (or the `role` the server passes), not from an authenticated identity; `actor` is a label. The server owns authentication.
- **A hand-written rewrite cannot keep a second clause at the superseded time.** After the appointment moved from Thursday 11:00 a.m., a delivered text that still names "Thursday" or "11:00 a.m." anywhere (e.g. "physio Thursday 11:00 a.m.") is refused: the gate cannot tell another appointment at the old time from the old appointment. A person writes that fact without the old weekday and clock, or a clinician source states it.
- **After a rejected instruction change that came from a wording correction** the instruction cites the corrected-away revision again (its last approved state) until its next change; the transcript correction itself is not undone, and the rewrite of its item cites the latest revision (`start_generation` follows it). The Reject button on such an instruction therefore leaves its item for a person to rewrite. When the correction changed the **time**, the **condition** or a **dose or negation of the action**, the Reject button refuses (409): the corrected transcript and the restored time would contradict each other and no rewrite could fit both; the way back is a counter-correction of the transcript or a clinician source. Rejecting a later change on top of such a correction keeps the correction (the instruction stays pending review at the corrected time, condition or action).
- **The fit check's condition rule is a word-stem heuristic** (half of the words the new condition adds, 3-letter stems, a stop list of pronouns and fillers): it catches a text that lacks the new condition or restates a rejected one, not a paraphrase with other words, which a person rewrites. It applies only to a condition a change introduced: an instruction whose condition never changed keeps its approved items as they are. After a transcript correction of the condition's words, the new word is required only where it replaced a required word ('your glasses' -> 'your hearing aid'), and a corrected-away word is refused only while no current source states it (a correction of 'follow-up appointment' to 'follow-up visit' does not forbid the word 'appointment' that other sentences still state).
- **A changed dose or negation is a person's review, not a substitution.** A superseding proposal that changes a number or negation of the action makes the instruction pending with the new action and leaves every item resting on it for a person's rewrite; the action rule of the fit check reads numbers (digits and number words) and the presence of a negating word, not their meaning; a negating word in a text that restates an action must be stated by the action, its condition or its sentence, so a text that phrases a positive instruction with a negation its sources do not state ("do not let her get out of bed alone" for "ask the caregiver for assistance before getting out of bed") is refused and is written positively instead. A discontinuation is entered as a negated action ('do not take ...'; the instruction stays active); there is no decision that withdraws an approved instruction. A transcript correction that adds or removes a negation in a sentence whose action states it in other words, or changes more than one number of it, is refused: the changed action then comes from a clinician source.
- **A corrected time is read from the corrected clause only.** The clause is the source sentence that names both the instruction's weekday and clock; a sentence that states them in two sentences is corrected through the time itself (the corrected words applied to it), and corrected words that state two weekdays or clocks are refused.
- **Approve-all leaves a version that no longer fits pending** (listed in `reviewed.refused`): a person rejects it (its approved text comes back if it fits) or delivers a rewrite. A state persisted by an older engine (a version approved before this check existed) is judged at the export (`ExportBlocked`) and rewritten the same way.
- **The HTTP server does not pass the reasons on yet:** `ExportBlocked` reaches the client as `409 pending_review` with the ids, and `NotFit` as `409` with `type NotFit` and the reasons in the message; `reviewed.refused` is in the event.
- **Confirmation roles fail closed, and are conservative on purpose.** What the primary caregiver may confirm on the clinician's behalf is an allow-list: an instruction whose action is one of two non-medication templates (attend an appointment; bring a document or an aid), with a condition of appointment words only, either new or moved in time only; the item versions that rest only on such instructions and add no word, number, negation, stop word or medication verb their approved text and instructions do not state; and the fall note and its task. The engine does not know what a drug is: it guarantees only that nothing outside those templates is confirmable, so every medication (also a brand or a drug name no list has), every dose and every condition change goes to the clinician, also when it is harmless (a reworded condition, a medication time, a rewrite with a new word). The word lists (medication words, number words, stop words, exception words) are a second layer on top of the allow-list, not the rule. Roles come from the fixture's `roles` map and `primary_caregiver` setting, not from an authenticated identity. **Every `at` must be stamped by the server** as a naive local minute (each change, review, upload and mark; `dt.datetime.now().strftime('%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M')`, not a time with a UTC offset, which the engine refuses): the engine has no clock, refuses an `at` with a timezone, never counts `uploaded_at` and never lets a change's pending time be earlier than the latest accepted `at`, but a client that may choose the confirmation's own `at` could still claim the time limit has passed. The video and the demo keep the fixture's times (care_cases.md, video path).
- Which instruction a question is about is a word-overlap heuristic (`_relevant_instruction`); a question that names none of the instruction's words may be matched to the wrong one, which only changes which deterministic reply is used.
